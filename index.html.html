Common Cyberattacks Related to Passwords

1. Brute Force Attack: Tries every possible password combination until the correct one is found.
2. Dictionary Attack: Uses a list of common words and passwords to guess credentials.
3. Credential Stuffing: Uses stolen username/password pairs from previous breaches.
4. Phishing: Tricks users into revealing passwords via fake emails or websites.
5. Keylogging: Records keystrokes to capture passwords as they are typed.

Requirements

  • At least 8 characters
  • A lowercase letter
  • An uppercase letter
  • A number
  • A symbol

How Hackers Guess Passwords

Hackers don’t guess one by one — they use tools that try millions of combinations per second. They run through leaked password lists (“123456,” “password,” names, football teams), then tweak them with rules like adding “123” or swapping “a” for “@” — which is why “P@ssw0rd!” falls almost as fast as “password.” They also try every character combination (brute force) and reuse passwords leaked from other sites (credential stuffing). Short or common passwords fall in seconds; long, unique passphrases can take centuries. Length and uniqueness beat complexity tricks every time.
